# Independent validation protocol

Status: **planned; no validation results recorded here**. Protocol revision: 12 September 2026.
Implements execution-plan F4–F6. Freeze the revision before collecting each evaluation
batch; retain failures and later protocol amendments.

## Questions this study must answer

1. Can an intended coordinator complete a real, authorized task with mobilize?
2. Does its policy offer a useful improvement over a serious alternative under that
   coordinator's constraints, and what does the improvement cost?
3. Can someone unfamiliar with the project understand the result and handle a failure?
4. Can an independent reviewer trace the demonstration to its actual implementation?

These are different questions. A successful scripted call cannot answer all four.

## Participant and session record

For each session record a pseudonymous participant/session ID, participant role,
relationship to the builder, date, product commit, protocol revision, domain, scenario,
authorization, permitted publication scope, and whether the activity was scripted.
Keep contact details and private consent records out of the public artifact.

Recruit at least one intended practitioner for workflow evaluation and two people who
did not build the system for independent usability/reproduction checks. These are
internal coverage targets, not statistical proof of adoption or a sponsor requirement.
Record missing participation as missing evidence. Do not substitute agent role-play
for independent human validation.

## Part A: practitioner problem and workflow check

Before demonstrating the product, ask the practitioner to describe a recent relevant
coordination task: target, deadline, roster source, calling sequence, acceptable excess
commitments, follow-up, and how they know the job is done. Record the operational
constraint that would make mobilize useful or unacceptable.

Then ask them to complete the equivalent task with the application. Observe rather
than narrate each step. Record interventions, misunderstandings, missing information,
and the action they would take next. Ask what would prevent using it again.

Publish, with permission: context, observed task, result, limitations, and product
changes attributable to the feedback. A quote is supporting evidence, not the outcome.
Do not present willingness to try as actual adoption.

## Part B: controlled end-to-end provider session

Use consenting participants and an accurately described test or low-risk activity.
Freeze intended utterance categories separately from observed labels. Include firm
agreement, conditional agreement, retraction, opt-out and failed/no contact. Include
positive controls. Real calls require a separately authorized recipient list/budget.

For every operation link:

`session → application revision → request/schema → task ID → attempt ID → terminal
payload → transcript → independent final-intent label → application verdict → wave
decision → final roster`

Record attempts, successful connections, retries, latency and charges when available.
Never infer charges from an old per-call rate. Retain failed attempts; report the
numerator and denominator for every accuracy or success claim.

Label original real interaction, captured real-payload replay and synthetic fixture
separately. A replay can test a new decision implementation; it cannot establish that
a changed call prompt or provider request works on a new real conversation.

## Part C: observed outcome session

For an actual authorized volunteer/shift activity, record final commitments and later
attendance, including observation source/time. Preserve pending and unknown outcomes.
Avoid fabricated emergencies or claims that a test participant was a recruited donor.

Report invitations, contacted people, commitments, arrivals, excess arrivals,
missed need, and coordinator follow-up effort. An initial pilot establishes only what
was observed in that pilot. Do not infer calibrated probabilities from a tiny sample.

If comparing workflows, define comparable tasks and constraints before collection;
avoid calling the same person repeatedly to manufacture a paired experiment. Use a
valid historical baseline or separate authorized sessions, disclose confounders, and
keep causal claims limited. Simulation remains the controlled policy comparison.

## Part D: blinded policy comparison

Follow B1's shared-outcome protocol. Independently preselect scenarios and practical
thresholds from the operational constraints before seeing policy results. An evaluator
should receive neutral policy names where feasible. Include same-scoring sequential
and fixed-wave competitors. Report all planned scenarios, uncertainty and adverse cases.

A useful result is a defensible operating region or evidence that a simpler policy is
preferable. Do not search for a flattering seed and call that measured superiority.
Distinguish measured real-call latency/cost, modeled duration, and hypothetical savings.

## Part E: novice product and judge-comprehension check

Give each independent participant the published instructions without a live walkthrough.
Ask them to:

1. Launch the no-call experience and explain its mode.
2. Run a success scenario and explain commitments versus actual attendance.
3. Inspect a refusal and identify the evidence behind it.
4. Run an unresolved-call/shortage scenario and choose the safe next action.
5. Locate the exact source revision and reproduce one evidence item.

Record completion, interventions, incorrect interpretations and elapsed time. After
watching the final video once, ask who the user is, what task completed, what CALL-E
did, why the decision was defensible, and what remains uncertain. Do not supply answers.

A mistaken belief that simulated calls were real, commitments were arrivals, or
in-flight calls were canceled is a release-blocking communication defect. Fix the
product/copy and repeat the affected check with a fresh participant where possible.

## Reporting and release

Publish a results document only after collection. Include protocol revision, product
revision, counts, exclusions with reasons, raw redacted evidence links, failures,
changes, retest results and unresolved limitations. Separate qualitative observations
from quantitative estimates. An independent reviewer signs off on traceability, not
on a promise of winning.
